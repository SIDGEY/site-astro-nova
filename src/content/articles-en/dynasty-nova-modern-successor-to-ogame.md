## A next-generation OGame

Dynasty Nova keeps the fundamentals that made OGame a hit: planet management, economic development, technology research, fleet building, galactic exploration and colonization. Players who know Gameforge's classic will find their bearings quickly.

The main difference lies in the user experience. Where OGame now suffers from an aging interface, Dynasty Nova offers a modern, clean design built from the ground up for mobile devices. As soon as they log in, players get a 3D view of their planet, a visual touch that works particularly well.

## Familiar planet management, modernized

Like the game that inspired it, Dynasty Nova is built around mining a few main resources:

- Metal
- Crystal
- Hydrogen

To produce them, players have dedicated buildings such as the Mineral Excavator, the Crystal Extractor and the Hydrogen Condenser. Other facilities generate energy, increase storage capacity or improve the planet's overall performance.

The game also adds a few twists of its own, such as the Planetary Base, whose level has to be high enough to unlock certain upgrades.

All of this comes with detailed descriptions and a particularly polished interface that makes the game easy to pick up.

## Well-organized installations and research

Dynasty Nova also has installations comparable to OGame's:

- Innovation Center (the equivalent of the Research Lab)
- Automaton Factory (the equivalent of the Robotics Factory)
- Orbital Dock (the equivalent of the Shipyard)

Some features are still in development, which is normal at this stage of the project.

The tech tree is one of the interface's strong points. Unlike OGame, where research is often lumped into one long list, Dynasty Nova sorts it by category:

- Propulsion
- Advanced technologies
- Economic research
- Military developments

This layout makes progression much easier to read.

## Defenses and fleets: all the classics are there

The defense systems follow the traditional mechanics, with classic and advanced defensive structures, including several types of shields.

On the fleet side, players will find most of the familiar units under new names and with new looks. Their stats stay broadly close to OGame's, so the fleet still feels familiar.

Among the most impressive ships is the **Colossus stellaire**, which seems to play a role similar to OGame's Deathstar. Extremely expensive to build, it is one of the most ambitious endgame goals.

## A clearer galaxy and simpler espionage

The galaxy interface has also been given a thorough makeover.

Espionage, a core part of the gameplay, becomes much more intuitive. Reports show the essential information up front, without stacking menus and submenus. In a few clicks, players get a clear summary of the resources and infrastructure they spied on.

This simplification makes the game far more comfortable to play, especially for players who are used to analyzing lots of targets.

## Colonization and exploration

As in OGame, players can expand their empire by colonizing several planets.

A specific technology called **Astrophysics** lets you, among other things:

- Increase the number of colonies available.
- Unlock expeditions.
- Gradually extend your influence across the galaxy.

The mechanics deliberately stay close to the original model to keep a familiar strategic depth.

## A project still in development

It's worth remembering that Dynasty Nova is still unfinished. Several features are still being integrated, and some buildings or systems aren't fully operational yet.

At the moment, some test versions mainly offer PvE content, while the PvP features are still being developed.

The developers are nonetheless preparing a beta phase open to players who want to take part in testing. Community feedback and bug hunting are an important step before the official launch.

## A project worth following closely

Dynasty Nova isn't trying to revolutionize the genre so much as to modernize a formula that has long proven itself. By keeping the mechanics that won over millions of players while offering a more ergonomic interface suited to the way people play today, the project has a strong case to make.

For players nostalgic for OGame as well as newcomers looking for a space strategy game they can play in a browser, Dynasty Nova looks like a particularly promising initiative.

If development keeps heading in this direction, the game could well become one of the most interesting alternatives in the genre in the months ahead.
