## Introduction

Dynasty Nova opens the Station tab, which brings together three things in the order the story unfolds: the common purse buys the station, the station unlocks a progression, and that progression hands out points to spend in a doctrine tree. Here's how each layer works.

## The treasury, a one-way fund

### Giving from your planet, under a double cap

A member pays in resources from one of their planets, capped both by their own stock and by the room left in the treasury. There is no way to withdraw what has been paid in: a fund that could pay back out would become a channel for transfers between accounts, and would let players get around everything the game protects by keeping its purchases expensive.

### A balance for everyone, a ledger for a few

The treasury balance stays visible to every member, without exception. The detailed ledger of contributions, on the other hand, only shows for members who hold the right to manage the treasury: a donation ranking open to everyone would quickly turn each member's generosity into a source of internal disputes.

## The station, the alliance's first joint purchase

### A one-time purchase, with no vote

The station is built in one go, instantly, with no vote required, unlike an expensive doctrine talent, which may require one. The reason comes down to what's at stake: a vote protects against sabotage when choosing between several branches, whereas here there's only one thing to buy, funded by donations made precisely for it.

### Progress you read resource by resource

Progress toward the station's price is shown resource by resource, never as an overall percentage. An alliance at 100% of its metal but 20% of its hydrogen isn't “at 73%”: it's short on hydrogen, and that's what anyone about to donate needs to be told. The weekly ceilings on earnings are shown too, with an actual reset date rather than just a time frame.

## The doctrine tree: what the points buy

### The whole catalog on display, bought or not

The tree displays its entire catalog, including talents nobody has bought yet, because a progression tree has to answer “what are we working toward?” and not only “what do we already have?” Each talent shows its current value and what the next level would bring, for example from +10% to +15%. A talent that has never been bought shows a dash rather than a misleading “+0%”.

### Three concrete bonuses, capped so they never overflow

Among the talents already wired up, Shared holds raises the treasury's ceiling, while Coordinated propulsion and Optimised supply apply to all of a member's fleets, not just their grouped operations. Percentage bonuses add up rather than multiply from one level to the next, so they never exceed the cap set for their bonus family.

## Conclusion

The treasury, the station and the doctrine tree form a single loop: giving moves the alliance toward its next level, each level hands out points, and those points buy bonuses that benefit the whole alliance. One talent, mutual assistance between members, is already in the catalog but has no effect for now: its function will arrive along with the aid ceiling it has yet to unlock.
