## Introduction

For eight months, we developed Dynasty Nova, a free 4X space strategy game you play in your browser, designed mobile-first as a reaction to OGame's aging UX. Then, in the middle of the beta, we had to change its name. Here's why, and what it taught us about designing the identity of a game that's already live.

## A name already taken, without our knowing

### An unforgiving name clash

“Astro Nova” seemed like an original name when the project started. In reality, another game, an action roguelike released on Steam, has exactly the same name. It has nothing to do with the space strategy game we were building, but the confusion was very real for a player typing “Astro Nova” into a search engine.

### The risk of getting lost in another game

Two games with the same name isn't just a cosmetic annoyance: it means lost traffic, mixed-up reviews and, eventually, a risk of brand confusion that's no longer in our hands. Keeping the name meant building our visibility on foundations borrowed from someone else.

## Choosing a name that says more about the game

### Dynasty: building an empire over time

“Astro” only described the setting: space, generic and already crowded with similar games. “Dynasty” tells a different story: building an empire over time, managing it, growing a lineage game after game. It's closer to what players actually experience.

### Checking before you get attached to a name

This time, we checked upfront: the name's availability on search engines, the dynastynova.com domain, the social media accounts. A game's name is never just a word: it's also a URL, an app store ID, a hashtag. Checking it before you get attached saves you from going through a rebrand like this one a second time.

## What a rebrand really changes

### The gameplay stays put, the identity doesn't

The name changes, not the game. The resource management, the tech tree and the fleet system stay exactly the same (they're described in our overview of the [free space strategy browser game](/en/blog/free-space-strategy-browser-game-dynasty-nova/)). What changes is everything that carries the name: logo, palette, icons, descriptions.

### Migrating without losing the beta players

The real challenge wasn't graphic, it was human: warning the players already signed up on play-astronova.com, redirecting the old domain name, and communicating clearly so that no tester would think the project was shutting down. A silent rebrand, with no explanation, always looks like abandonment.

## Conclusion

A rebrand in the middle of a beta costs time we'd rather spend elsewhere. But it's better to do it early, while the player base is still small, than later, when every URL change breaks links, reviews and habits. Checking a name's availability before choosing it remains the simplest lesson, and the one indie creators most often overlook. Dynasty Nova is still the same space strategy game, born of frustration with OGame's aging UX: the name has changed, the ambition hasn't.
