## Introduction

When I started working on Dynasty Nova, my goal was clear: reinvent the user experience of an OGame-inspired space strategy game, with a modern, elegant and smooth mobile interface. But working alone on a project this big also means finding ways to streamline every step of the creative process. That's where artificial intelligence came in: not as a replacement, but as a creative assistant, a copilot serving my vision.

## Background

### It started with what I saw as a player

The project began with a letdown: seeing OGame's mobile version fall far short of the desktop experience. A squeezed interface, lost visual cues, players gradually tuning out… The potential was there, but the user experience no longer lived up to it. As both a player and a designer, I decided to roll the dice again and imagine a modernized, mobile-first version built on today's best practices.

### A solo project, limited resources, AI-assisted tools

Dynasty Nova isn't a team project. It's a personal project that I run on my own, in my spare time. To avoid drowning in the sheer amount of work, I chose to build artificial intelligence tools into my workflow from the very start. Not to automate design, but to assist me: to create, iterate and structure faster, and better.

## AI as a visual design assistant

### KREA: a junior designer… only faster

Right from the start, I used KREA to come up with the Dynasty Nova logo. I had the name and the universe, but I needed a visual signature. After generating more than 50 options from prompts, I landed on a symbolic, dynamic shape that suggests both a rocket and the letter A, almost an echo of Assassin's Creed.

But that starting point was still rough. So I handled it the way I would handle work delivered by a junior designer: vector refinement, smoothing out the curves, applying a typographic grid. The final result isn't a raw AI image but a true hybrid creation: AI suggests, the designer finishes.

### Generation guided by prompts and style

Beyond the logo, all of the game's visuals went through a similar process. I took OGame's assets (200x200 px), too small and too recognizable, and set out to transform them.

- An initial upscale with a specialized AI tool, up to 8x.
- A text description generated by AI (GPT-style) to write a suitable prompt.
- A custom style in KREA, fed with a consistent set of images so it could infer a visual signature.
- Batch generation from those prompts + style to create original visuals that fit the game without imitating the originals.

Each image then went through a post-processing pipeline: HD upscale (Freepik), compression (TinyPNG), WebP conversion.

## AI tools as a productivity toolbox

### Bolt: Tailwind mockups to lay the foundations

I used Bolt to quickly generate rough HTML/CSS interface drafts with Tailwind. Those drafts let me move fast, explore page structures and map out a first version of the navigation without having to prototype everything in Figma.

### Cursor and AI-powered IDEs: iterating without friction

With Cursor (or Windsurf), AI was built right into my IDE. That let me move from one component to the next faster, fix things live and generate more effective UI blocks. The result: less time spent on tiny code tweaks, more focus on the overall experience.

### From front end to Figma: rebuilding the foundations with HTML to Design

When I wanted to structure the interface in more detail, I used the HTML to Design plugin in Figma to import my HTML components as static mockups. Even though the result wasn't 100% clean, this approach let me quickly build a design system with reusable components, colors and styles.

## Manual finishing: staying in control of the result

### Making the AI visuals consistent and clean

No generated image was used as is. Every one was retouched: perspective fixes, artifact removal, realigned lighting. I also ran each image through Figma to adjust contrast and saturation, then copied and pasted those settings with plugins to keep everything consistent.

### Fitting each visual to its use

The generated visuals were square, but the game's cards are vertical (2:3 format). For each item (buildings, research, ships, shop…), I placed the image in a card, simulated the overflow and picked the one that worked best in the frame. It was a process of selection, but also of elimination, until only one image was left for each item.

## Conclusion

Using artificial intelligence doesn't mean giving up on creative work. On Dynasty Nova, AI was a lever, an engine, a catalyst, never an end in itself. It let me go faster, dare more, generate more… but always under my supervision.

In this context, AI plays the role of a hyper-productive junior creative: it suggests, it enriches, it speeds things up. But the designer's eye remains the key. Because what makes good design isn't just the idea or the style: it's the intent, the consistency, the experience you want to convey.
