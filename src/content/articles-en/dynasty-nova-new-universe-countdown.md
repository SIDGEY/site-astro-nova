## Introduction

Dynasty Nova now separates a universe's birth from its opening. A galaxy can be created, mapped and announced several days before anyone can set foot in it, with a countdown visible in-game until launch time. Here's what that changes, concretely, for a player watching for a new server to open.

## A universe that exists before it opens its gates

### The map is ready before the first players arrive

A scheduled universe generates its galaxy as soon as it's created, exactly like a universe that opens right away: systems, planets and positions are already in place on the day the announcement goes up. This rules out any last-minute work at launch, and lets the team present a real universe rather than just a promised date.

### It's in the list, but can't be joined

An announced universe appears in the universe list just like one that's already active; that's precisely what makes it possible to announce it in advance. Whether it can be joined never depends on its presence in that list: a dedicated field, invisible to the player, settles that question on its own. So a universe can be seen without being accessible, and the two pieces of information never get mixed up.

## The countdown, and nothing else

### An amber badge with the exact time

A universe waiting to launch carries an amber badge showing the time left before it opens, in place of its usual age. The countdown runs in the player's browser, second by second, but it never decides anything on its own: its job is to inform, not to open the door.

### The button unlocks on its own, no page reload

As the announced time approaches, the page regularly asks the server for a fresh universe list, and it's the server's answer that flips the Join button from locked to active. No page reload is needed: a player who keeps the tab open sees the button unlock before their eyes, right at the announced time. If their browser's clock runs a little fast, the badge simply says the opening is imminent, instead of showing a counter at zero next to a button that's still locked, which could have looked like a bug.

## What the team can do before launch

### Schedule, postpone, or open on the spot

When a universe is created, a launch date can be set: left empty, the universe opens immediately, as before. Once scheduled, its launch can be pushed back to a later date as long as it hasn't opened yet, and a dedicated button can open it on the spot if needed, simply by bringing the announced date forward to the present moment.

### A clear line once the universe is open

Rescheduling a launch is only possible for a universe that hasn't opened its gates yet. A universe that's already active keeps the date it actually opened on, as an established fact rather than a setting that can still be changed: the players who joined it are there, and that date is now part of their galaxy's history.

## Conclusion

Announcing a universe before it opens gives players time to plan their arrival, alliance first, instead of discovering a launch at the last minute. The countdown does the informing, the server does the deciding, and the line between the two leaves no room for ambiguity.
