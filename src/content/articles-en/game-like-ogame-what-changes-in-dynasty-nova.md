## Introduction

Dynasty Nova keeps OGame's fundamentals (resource management, research and fleets) but changes the mobile interface, how play time is balanced and how you get into combat.

## What stays familiar

### The same resources, the same colonization

Metal, crystal and hydrogen still pay for buildings, research and fleets, and colonizing new planets remains the same engine of expansion an OGame player recognizes right away.

### A research tree that gates everything

As in OGame, every advanced building or ship requires one or more technology prerequisites: there's no skipping steps without investing in the matching research.

## What actually changes

| Aspect | OGame | Dynasty Nova |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mobile interface | A late adaptation of an interface designed for desktop | Designed mobile-first from the design system up, before the desktop version |
| Business model | Classic paid speed-ups | Openly pay-to-fast: Stellar Points speed up a queue, with no permanent advantage |
| Rewarding time online | Being logged in nonstop remains a strong advantage | Rebalanced economy so a less available player stays competitive |
| Before attacking | No preview of a battle's outcome | Battle simulator running the real engine, win rate calculated over 50 runs |
| Communication | Classic private messaging | Live universe chat and a dedicated channel for each alliance |

### An interface designed for touch first

Where OGame's mobile interface is still an adaptation of a layout designed for desktop, Dynasty Nova builds its design system mobile-first: touch targets sized for a thumb, navigation rethought rather than squeezed.

### Pay-to-fast rather than pay-to-win

Spending Stellar Points lets you speed up a construction or research queue in progress, never get a ship or a technology a player couldn't have reached on their own: real money buys time, not power.

## Guidance OGame didn't offer at launch

### A quest system that guides your early game

Where a new OGame player often has to learn by trial and error or from outside guides, Dynasty Nova builds in a quest system that steers your progress from the very first hours, without leaving the game's interface.

### Espionage that sets up the attack instead of leaving it to chance

A spy report no longer just informs you: it feeds straight into the battle simulator, which replays the fight 50 times with the game's real engine before a single fleet leaves. That turns a gamble into a calculation.

## What doesn't change

### The strategic challenge is fully intact

Dynasty Nova promises neither easy wins nor an ultra-realistic simulation: every choice of building, research or expedition still weighs on your progress, exactly as in OGame.

## Conclusion

A player coming from OGame finds the same strategic backbone in Dynasty Nova, but on an interface designed for today and with an economy that no longer penalizes the players who are online the least.
