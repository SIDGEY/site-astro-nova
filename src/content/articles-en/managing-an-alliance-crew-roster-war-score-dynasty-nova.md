## What the new alliance screens show

Dynasty Nova's alliance screens have been rebuilt to answer, at a glance, the questions an alliance leader asks every day: **who's active, who's progressing, who can do what, and where the war stands**. They borrow the shop's visual language, with tiles, rank colors and short explanations.

### The crew roster

The Members tab is now a roster of tiles. Each member appears with their portrait on a color gradient that reflects their rank in the alliance: gold for the founder, then the rarity colors for vice-president, officer, member and recruit. A ribbon shows their place in the universe's general ranking.

### Sorting by what you're looking for

The roster can be sorted by rank, ranking, seniority, name or activity, and a second click reverses the order. The sort is kept in the page address, so you can share a sorted view directly with an officer.

## Knowing who still answers

An alliance is only as strong as the members who show up when an enemy fleet is closing in. The roster finally makes that information visible.

### Each member's last signal

A glowing “In orbit” dot marks a member who was active in the last 20 minutes. Past that, the roster reads “Last signal 3 hours ago” and then, after a week away, “Radio silence” with a grayed-out portrait. Counters at the top of the roster sum up how many members are online and how many have gone silent.

### Information for the alliance only

That last signal only appears on your own alliance's roster. The general ranking still doesn't show it: an opponent can't use it to spot unguarded empires.

## Readable diplomacy and wars

The redesign also covers diplomacy, votes and administration, where decisions commit the whole alliance.

### Live war scores

Each ongoing war is shown as a head-to-head, with the number of battles won by each side in the middle. Until now, that score could only be checked once the war was over. Pacts appear as cards with their betrayal cost and their notice period, and votes show a live projection, along the lines of “Adopted if nothing changes.”

### The “Who can do what” matrix

The Rights tab crosses twelve powers with the alliance's five ranks, along with the functions that also grant each of them. Any member can look at it, which cuts down on repeated questions in the chat and makes responsibilities clear before an operation.

## Why an alliance changes the game

In a multiplayer space strategy game, the alliance is what turns an isolated empire into a power that counts: shared missions, a common purse, doctrine and mutual defense.

### Missions and station, better explained

Alliance missions now appear as cards showing their difficulty, the collective progress and the Bronze, Silver and Gold tiers. The station shows its level, its doctrines and the treasury gauges.
