## Introduction

Dynasty Nova is opening its alliance system. Found a group of players, set its recruitment policy, hand out ranks, and measure yourself against other alliances in a dedicated ranking: the complete foundation arrives in a single wave.

## Founding an alliance

### A two-tier tech requirement

Founding an alliance requires a level 3 Logistics Center and the Stellar Diplomacy research at level 1. That research in turn requires a level 5 Innovation Center, level 4 Quantum Computing and, from now on, a level 3 Logistics Center: an adjustment that keeps players from discovering the real price of entry only when they go to found their alliance, after already investing in the research alone.

### Free, but not without constraints

Founding an alliance no longer costs any resources. The other safeguards stay in place: the required Logistics Center and research levels, a cooldown after leaving an alliance, and a single alliance seat per universe. How many members the alliance can take in still depends on the Stellar Diplomacy level reached.

## An identity for every alliance

### Standing out without waiting for an artist

The founding screen offers 12 colors, 8 emblems and 6 banners, built from icons and gradients rather than custom illustrations. That's enough to give every alliance a recognizable identity without depending on dedicated artwork.

### A public profile anyone can view

Every alliance has a public profile page: identity, leaders, ranks, and the history of the names it has gone by. Renaming an alliance is already wired into the interface but can't be completed for now: the cost of the operation depends on an alliance treasury that doesn't exist yet.

## Governing your group

### Rights live on each seat, not on the rank

Nothing is inferred from rank on the interface side: each member carries their own permissions on their row of the roster. A recruit who has been given only the Recruiter function can therefore change the recruitment policy, even though no other row of the roster opens up for them.

### Recruitment and settling in

The recruitment policy is set in one click, with three modes to choose from: open, by application, or by invitation. A new member then goes through a settling-in period with a countdown, during which they can leave or be excluded.

### A succession that doesn't depend on anyone

A founder who goes silent for 15 days triggers an automatic succession of command, flagged by a warning banner while it resolves. The alliance's own chat channel, locked since the chat launched, finally opens to all members.

## Measuring up against other alliances

### A ranking of its own, not a fifth tab

The alliance ranking lives on its own screen rather than inside the player ranking: the two ladders don't measure the same thing. Its Power tab adds up, live, every member's building, research and fleet points. A Progression tab already exists but says plainly that it can't be calculated yet, since there is no alliance level or experience at this stage.

### Deliberate caution, down to the details

Even the founder spends 24 hours in the settling-in period after founding, with no exception for rank. It's a way of reminding everyone that an alliance is built over time, never in the moment right after it's founded.

## Conclusion

Alliances lay the foundation for a more collective game: founding, governing, recruiting, ranking. Pacts, wars and alliance levels are still to come, but the structure that will host them is already in the game.
