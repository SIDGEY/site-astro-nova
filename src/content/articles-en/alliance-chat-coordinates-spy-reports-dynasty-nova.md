## Introduction

An alliance's chat channel now accepts typed cards: a galaxy coordinate, a shared spy report, soon a battle. So you can coordinate a group without copying numbers into a message by hand.

## Cards that point, never reveal

### A reference, never the content

Each card shows an icon, the name of its type and the reference as a player reads it: a plain coordinate or a report's short ID, never a title, a figure or an excerpt of the target. The content can only be read by opening the card, on the target's own screen, which checks at that very moment whether the reader is allowed to see it.

### One message for every dead end

A target that has been deleted, is out of the reader's reach or belongs to a type that doesn't exist yet gets exactly the same treatment: the card grays out, shows “Content unavailable”, and the thread carries on uninterrupted.

### Report sharing comes first

Six card types are already declared, each with its own icon and destination, but only spy report sharing has a screen to post one today. The battle card and the coordinate card will follow in an upcoming release.

## Sharing a spy report without losing control of it

### One button, one comment, nothing else to choose

A spy report's detail view now has a Share button, which opens a dialog with an optional comment. The planet's name and coordinates are deliberately not copied into the message: they would outlive the shared report there, and could end up contradicting it.

### A borrowed report stays a borrowed report

A member who opens a report shared by someone else sees it labeled as such, and loses the actions reserved for the original recipient: no sharing it in turn, no deleting it, not even being marked as read automatically. Simulating a battle or sending a fleet against the scouted target remain available, though, since they act on the reader's own forces.

## Stay informed without watching the alliance screen all the time

### The alliance journal joins reports and quests

The existing notification center gains a third source: the alliance journal. A rank change, a succession alert or an accepted application now shows up there, with a read or unread status that comes straight from the server instead of being guessed by the interface.

### What doesn't come through yet

A rejected application doesn't notify its author, for lack of a per-player notification system outside an alliance. Votes, wars, mutual aid and missions don't exist yet: for now, their future actions will fall back on generic wording rather than stay invisible.

## Conclusion

With typed cards, report sharing and the alliance journal, Dynasty Nova gives alliances a real space for coordination, designed never to expose more than each reader is allowed to see.
