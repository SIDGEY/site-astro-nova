## Introduction

Dynasty Nova is opening war votes between alliances. An alliance's leadership puts the question forward, its officers vote, and adoption automatically triggers the declaration, without any single player being able to decide on their own.

## Voting for war instead of declaring it alone

### The right to propose is narrower than the right to vote

An alliance's Diplomat proposes a war question, but the body that votes is broader: every officer can weigh in, even without the rank needed to open the question themselves. Rank governs who can propose, never who gets to decide.

### Adoption publishes the declaration on its own

The question passes by a majority of the votes cast, with no quorum to reach, and a tie means it's rejected. There is no route for declaring war directly: only an adopted vote issues the declaration command, which makes the decision collective by design rather than through a check someone could forget to write.

### Early closing, protected by a one-minute guard

The vote count stays visible while the question runs. A question can be closed early, but the early close is logged in the history right alongside the result. A one-minute guard prevents a question from being opened and closed again straight away on its author's vote alone, since there is no quorum.

## What declaring war costs

### Breaking a pact has a price

Declaring war on an alliance you're bound to by a pact is still allowed: the declaration ends the pact on the spot, but it costs a fair-play penalty, 10 points for a broken truce and 15 for a betrayed alliance pact. Denouncing the pact and then waiting out the 24-hour notice remains the free option; declaring right away leaves a stain.

### A decision that never rests on one player

A voted war doesn't single out any player. The betrayal doesn't go on the record of the Diplomat who put the question forward, since it was the leadership as a whole that decided.

## Living through a war, then ending it

### Three states, never confused

A war makes it clear whether you chose it or had it forced on you, and each side keeps its own stated motive on display: the two have no reason to look alike. In the galaxy, an enemy now carries a dedicated badge, but can be attacked exactly like a stranger.

### Peace takes two signatures, never one

Ending a war isn't something one side can improvise: peace is offered and signed by both sides, with no unilateral surrender possible. A war that ended because an alliance was dissolved says so explicitly, rather than passing itself off as a negotiated peace.

### No winner declared

The tally of a past war stays viewable on the alliance's profile, with battles counted for each side as they happen. No winner is singled out: a war doesn't produce one, and that's a deliberate choice rather than an oversight.

## Conclusion

Alliance wars turn Dynasty Nova's diplomacy into a truly collective theater: nobody can drag their alliance into a conflict alone anymore, and nobody can end one alone either. It remains to be seen which alliance will be the first to dare put the question to a vote.
