## What's leaving the shop

The Dynasty Nova shop is losing its rewarded ad card: this block offered players a small free reward in exchange for watching an ad. It's gone both from the “Free rewards” block of the Credits tab on mobile and from the equivalent row on desktop.

### A purely subtractive change

Nothing fills the space the card leaves behind: the shop screen simply refocuses on the resource packs already on offer, with nothing added to compensate. It's a deliberate choice to keep the screen readable rather than fill a gap with a new element.

### A consistent removal across all three languages

The text tied to this block disappears at the same time from the shop's French, English and Spanish translations, so that no trace of the feature lingers in one language but not another.

## Why remove a source of free rewards

A rewarded ad card usually exists to give players one more way to get resources without spending money. Removing it might seem to go against that goal, but the choice is part of a broader logic.

### A shop that's easier to read

Fewer elements on one screen means a quicker decision for the player: on the mobile Credits tab in particular, every extra block makes it harder to compare the packs on offer. Removing a block that wasn't central to the game's strategic progression makes the screen simpler to take in.

### The code stays in place, ready to return

The technical component that displayed the card isn't deleted from the game, only taken off the screen. That keeps open the option of bringing rewarded ads back later without starting from scratch, if the team decides the mechanic belongs somewhere else in the experience.

## What it changes for players in the game

In practice, opening the shop today takes you straight to the metal, crystal, hydrogen and Stellar Points packs, without a detour through an ad block before you get to what matters.

### More direct access to resource packs

A player who opens the shop to top up their resource stock gets there faster, with no in-between block to skip past. It's a modest but real usability gain on a screen players check often during a game.

### A shop that stays true to the game's logic

This removal follows the same direction as the previous shop redesigns: fewer decorative or secondary elements, more clarity about what really moves an empire forward. Exploring the galaxy remains a matter of strategy, not of ads to watch.
