## Introduction

A multiplayer space strategy game lives on its opponents. Start a game with three or four friends and the galaxy soon feels empty. In **Dynasty Nova**, a free OGame-inspired space strategy browser game, the owner of a private universe can now populate it with **bots**: computer-run empires, at the difficulty level they choose.

## Bots that play like players

### Computer-run empires

A Dynasty Nova bot is not a sitting target. It gets its home planet the same way a player does, then builds, colonizes and attacks. Open universes already have them: there, bots share the map with human players.

### Bots that live in alliances

Bots also band together in alliances. They donate to the treasury, sign up for alliance missions, offer pacts to other bot alliances and vote to retaliate when someone declares war on them. Against them, diplomacy matters as much as your fleet.

## Populating your private universe

### Right from creation

A private universe is a universe that runs on your rules, which your friends join with an [invitation code](/en/blog/private-universe-invite-code-dynasty-nova/). Its creation wizard gains a step, **Bots**, between the rules and the opening: that's where you set how many there are and how hard they play. Bots come on top of the players without taking anyone's seat, and arrive as soon as the map is ready. If their number risks leaving players short of space, the map grows.

### Mid-game

The owner's console gains a **Bots** tab. Each bot appears there with its profile, its points and its planets. You can add more, pause them (they stop playing until you resume them) or remove one. Removing a bot asks for confirmation, because it's final: its planets are abandoned, its fleets in flight are lost and it leaves its alliance.

## Three difficulty levels

### What each level changes

| Level | The opponents | Their start |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Easy | Mostly miners, who rarely attack | From scratch, kept to the players' size |
| Intermediate | The mix of the official universes: raiders, opportunists and miners | From scratch, kept to the players' size |
| Expert | Mostly raiders, who grow freely | With an empire already built |

### One setting for everyone

Changing the level mid-game also applies to the bots already there. A group of beginners can start on Easy to learn how to build their economy, then move up to Intermediate when the first fleets leave the docks. Expert is for genre veterans who want to be pushed hard from day one.

## Solo, with friends or in an open universe

### An opponent at any hour

Bots fix a well-known problem with games among friends: even when nobody is online, the galaxy keeps moving. There's always a neighbor to spy on, an empire to raid or an alliance to negotiate a pact with.

### Starting in an open universe

If you want to try the game, open universes are free and already have their bots. Every new player there gets [seven days of protection](/en/blog/new-player-protection-seven-day-shield-dynasty-nova/) to set up their economy, and the [game overview](/en/blog/free-space-strategy-browser-game-dynasty-nova/) sums up what awaits you.

With bots, a galaxy of a few friends becomes a populated universe, at the difficulty level you choose and can change at any time.
