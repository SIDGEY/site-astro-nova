## Introduction

Dynasty Nova lets you choose, report by report, what your empire receives and what the bell announces. When ten probes fly home, you don't always feel like reading the debrief: the Reports section of your preferences lets you decide.

## Choosing the reports you receive

### A dedicated section in your preferences

The settings live in **Settings**, on the **Preferences** tab, in the **Reports** section. Each row has a **Report** toggle, which decides whether you receive that report, and a **bell**, which decides whether it's announced. A summary counts the reports you receive and the ones that are announced.

### Three groups to find your way around

The rows are sorted into three groups: **Combat and espionage**, **Fleet missions** and **Fleet returns**. The game's other notifications, such as quests, stay in their own section.

### Receive first, announce second

A report you don't receive **is no longer written at all**: it appears neither on the Reports page nor in the bell, and its bell is switched off. A report you do receive can arrive silently, if you only turn off its announcement.

## What you always receive

### Espionage and combat

**Spy** and **battle** reports can't be turned down. The first is the very outcome of your mission; the second can be shared with your alliance. Only their announcement in the bell can be turned off.

### Incidents are never silenced

Even with its report turned off, an **incident** is always flagged: a fleet lost on its way back, a failed colonization or stationing, a vanished expedition. Silence applies to routine, not to bad news.

## Turning off fleet returns, mission by mission

### Eight missions, eight settings

**Fleet returns** are set separately for espionage, attack, transport, recycling, expedition, stationing, colonization and moon destruction. Each row shows its mission's report icon, so you can tell at a glance which fleet is coming home.

### The probe example

You can let your probes come home without a return report while keeping the one for your raids, with their loot. To understand the bell itself, our article on the [notification center](/en/blog/dynasty-nova-notification-center-seen-vs-read/) explains the difference between seen and read.

## Clearer reports

### A trip with both ends

Espionage, colonization, stationing, return, recycling and transport reports now name the **departure** base as well as the place of arrival. A flight report thus tells the story of the whole trip.

### Planet or moon

Every report says whether the place in question is a **planet** or a **moon**. The two share the same coordinates, and they weren't always easy to tell apart.

### The reason for a failure, under the verdict

When a mission fails, the reason appears under the verdict, on the report page. A return that finds its home base unavailable explains that the cargo is lost along with the fleet. A report's details open in a **window above the list**, and the battle log can be collapsed round by round. To prepare your fights, you can also [simulate a battle before sending your fleet](/en/blog/battle-simulator-test-your-fleet-dynasty-nova/).

## Conclusion

Dynasty Nova brings order to your empire's mail: the essentials always get through, and the rest is yours to set as you like.
