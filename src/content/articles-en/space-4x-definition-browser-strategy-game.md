## What is a 4X game?

**A 4X is a strategy game built around four verbs: explore, expand, exploit, exterminate.** Hence the name: eXplore, eXpand, eXploit, eXterminate. In a space 4X, those four verbs play out on a galactic scale: you map star systems, colonize planets, exploit their resources and take on other empires.

### Where the term comes from

The term goes back to journalist Alan Emrich, who used it in the September 1993 issue of *Computer Gaming World*, in his preview of **Master of Orion**. He gave the game an “XXXX” rating, a nod to the genre's four pillars.

### A genre broader than its name

The fourth X is misleading: nothing forces you to wipe out all your rivals. Modern 4X games, from **Civilization** to **Stellaris**, add diplomacy, research and economy, and are often won by means other than conquest.

## The four pillars, one by one

Each pillar asks the player a different question.

### Explore and expand

Exploring means knowing what surrounds you: free planets, neighbors, threats. Expanding means turning that knowledge into territory, by colonizing new worlds before anyone else does.

### Exploit and exterminate

Exploiting means running the economy: every planet produces resources that pay for buildings, research and fleets. Exterminating is the military side, which comes late, costs a lot, and can often be avoided through diplomacy.

## Space 4X in the browser

Browser strategy games in the OGame tradition keep these four pillars but change how time works.

### A universe that never stops

A classic 4X is turn-based, played alone or with a few others, over a match of a few hours. A browser game is played in a persistent, real-time universe: a construction takes real minutes, a fleet takes real hours to arrive, and the universe keeps going while you're away.

### More players, less time per session

Your opponents are other players, not an AI, and you log in for a few minutes several times a day rather than for a whole evening. Some games have stripped things down even further, like [Neptune's Pride](/en/blog/neptunes-pride-the-space-game-that-removed-the-interface/).
