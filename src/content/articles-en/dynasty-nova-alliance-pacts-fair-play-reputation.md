## Introduction

Dynasty Nova gives alliance diplomacy a real screen of its own: pacts in force, pending proposals and a history of violations, each displayed separately so you never confuse what the alliance has done to its partners with what it has suffered from them.

## A diplomacy screen everyone can see and few can act on

### Reading for everyone, action for diplomats

Every member of the alliance can open the diplomacy screen, since signed pacts and fair-play reputation concern everyone: they're public, and any player can read them from the ranking. Only diplomats get the buttons to propose, accept or denounce a pact, a right that comes from the server rather than from a rank recalculated on the client side.

### Two logs that never mix

What the alliance has done to its partners and what it has suffered from them appear in two separate lists, never merged into a single feed. You read them for opposite reasons, and mixing them up would have been the worst mistake this screen could make. Pact proposals follow the same logic: only one of the two directions offers an Accept button.

## A denounced pact that still binds

### The 24-hour notice, in black and white

A denounced pact stays in force for another 24 hours. The pact card says so explicitly instead of just changing color, and denouncing takes a second click whose only job is to confirm that notice period and its cost if you attack before it ends.

### A warning before every strike, never a block

A partner can still be identified in the galaxy by a badge that differs from a regular ally's, without any attack mission disappearing as a result: a pact remains a declaration, never a technical lock. A strike against a partner shows a warning naming the pact involved and its exact cost before departure, because the violation is recorded the moment the fleet leaves and recalling it refunds nothing.

## Reputation as a visible currency

### A badge the score alone can't give

The fair-play badge appears on the alliance's public profile and on every line of the ranking, where it answers a question the score can't settle on its own: the most powerful alliance in the universe and the one whose word is worth nothing can be the very same alliance.

### Missing rather than wrong

The badge may be missing on older alliances whose profile hasn't received this data yet. That gap is deliberate: better no information at all than a badge shown wrongly on a screen that doesn't receive the data yet.

## Conclusion

By making pacts and fair-play reputation readable everywhere without ever turning them into a lock, Dynasty Nova lets alliance diplomacy remain a strategic choice rather than a technical constraint. Now it's up to each alliance to decide what its word will really be worth.
