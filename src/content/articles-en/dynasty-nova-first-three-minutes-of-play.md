## Introduction

The first three minutes of a game often decide whether a player stays or closes the tab. Here is how Dynasty Nova designed its first three screens, before the player has placed a single building.

## Minute 0: choosing a universe, not just a server

### A visual identity for each universe

The first screen doesn't ask for a username or a password: it asks you to choose a universe from a list (Pegasus, Orion, Quantum, Vega…), each showing its player count and game length. Each universe also has its own visual identity (an amber nebula for Pegasus, a violet black hole for Orion, an icy galaxy for Quantum) to help you tell options apart at a glance where other games of the genre offer little more than a list of interchangeable server names.

### Numbers shown before you decide

This choice has real consequences for the pace of a game, and Dynasty Nova would rather show them upfront than let players find out after the fact. Each universe card shows what matters for the decision, not just a flattering name.

## Minute 1: the universe page, everything laid out before you commit

### The speed multiplier, never hidden

Tapping a universe brings up a detailed page, and only from there can the player click “Start the adventure.” Three numbers structure the screen: the player count, the game length, and a speed multiplier (economy, research). That multiplier sets the pace of the whole game (a player with two hours a day doesn't want the same universe as one who logs in once a week), and Dynasty Nova deliberately makes it the most visible piece of information on the decision screen, rather than a detail tucked away in a submenu.

### Balancing atmosphere and data

The page also shows a short blurb that gives the universe an identity rather than a bare table of numbers. The balance we're after is precise: enough atmosphere to make you want to play, enough data to decide without getting it wrong. Two universes with the same speed but different blurbs don't promise the same game.

## Minute 2: landing on your first planet

### The whole map in view from the first second

Once the universe is confirmed, the player lands directly on their starting planet: no loading screen, no cutscene. Resources and the entire navigation (Buildings, Research, Defense, Fleet) are already visible in the bottom bar. Rather than unlocking tabs one by one over the course of a tutorial, Dynasty Nova shows from the start where every system lives: a player who explores out of curiosity grasps the whole structure of the game faster.

### One possible action, not ten

On that first planet, only one construction is actually available on the very first click. The player isn't choosing between ten buildings to unlock; they see one that makes sense at that exact moment. It's a deliberate decision: hold back the advanced options rather than pile them all onto the first screen.

## Why there's no classic step-by-step tutorial

### Informing on the decision screen rather than guiding step by step

Most browser games put the player through a series of guided steps before letting them loose, a tutorial that teaches the basics and then leaves them on their own just as the real strategy begins. Dynasty Nova chose the opposite: less explanatory text, more information shown directly on the decision screens (speed, length, population, the universe's visual identity).

### A bet we're still fine-tuning

This choice isn't perfect yet: some complete beginners ask for more guidance, and we adjust the dial with every wave of feedback. It's too early, statistically, to claim it improves one-month retention, but the number of support questions about “how to get started” has dropped noticeably since these three screens went in.

## Conclusion

Dynasty Nova's first three minutes aren't there to impress, they're there to inform: which universe, at what pace, on what map. Three screens, three numbers to understand, and no surprise hidden behind a menu you'd only discover three weeks later.
